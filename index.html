A trading desk that researches, tests and trades on its own.

I built Hermes, a system that runs 24/7 without me in the loop. It collects strategy ideas, backtests them by the thousand, lets only the survivors trade on paper, and puts the best on funded trading accounts under strict risk rules. Every step is automated, and every promotion has to get past rules written in code.

How it runs, end to end

Six stages run on a schedule, every day. People decide the rules and approve real capital; the machine does the rest.

  1. Collect ideas

    Strategies come in from academic papers, published TradingView scripts, YouTube strategy channels and an AI that writes its own hypotheses.

    Each idea is turned into runnable TradingView code automatically, then queued for testing.

  2. Test at scale

    A robot drives TradingView, sweeping every idea across grids of settings and across markets: gold, indices, crypto, oil and currencies. Around a hundred sweeps finish every day.

    Promising near-misses are re-tested on a finer grid automatically, at most twice.

  3. Pass the gates

    A candidate needs at least 200 trades, a profit factor of 1.10 or better, drawdown under 10% and real trading costs priced in. It is then re-run on its full history, and refused if its last six months are fading.

    The gatekeeper is code. Even the AI that manages the pipeline cannot argue its way past it.

  4. Trade on paper

    Survivors get a live TradingView alert. Every signal is traded on paper against real prices, so each strategy builds a forward record before it touches money.

    A nightly monitor retires paper strategies whose forward results break from their backtest.

  5. Trade live

    The strongest strategies run on funded prop-firm accounts through MetaTrader 5. Each account is an isolated seat with its own risk limit and daily loss floor.

    One strategy per slot, and never opposite positions on the same market across accounts.

  6. Review and learn

    An AI chief executive reviews the whole pipeline twice a day, promotes what passed and escalates the rest. Every closed live trade is summarised on Telegram.

    Lessons are written down, and refusals are remembered so the same mistake is not re-proposed.

A tour of the dashboard

This is the control room I use every day. The tabs below show the six parts that matter most. Each clip is the real dashboard; open the live copy to click around yourself.

The funded accounts, live

Six prop-firm challenge accounts, each its own seat. You see every open position with its stop and target, each account's equity, and how much room is left before a firm rule would be broken.

Seats never share credentials or positions, and each trade is sized from the cash it would lose at its stop, so one bad trade can't sink an account.

Every live trading day

Days follow the prop firm's own day boundary, not midnight, so these numbers match exactly what the firm checks its rules against.

How I built it

I built Hermes alone, working with AI the way a lead works with a team. Here is the honest split.

What I did

  • Designed the architecture: the stages, the gates, the risk rules and how accounts stay isolated.
  • Wrote the rules every agent works inside, including what AI may never do on its own.
  • Decide what gets built next, approve every change to live capital, and rule on escalations.
  • Run it daily: diagnose production incidents, read the reports, retire what isn't working.

What AI did

  • Claude Code wrote most of the code under my direction and handles maintenance sessions.
  • Scheduled AI agents run the reviews: the chief-executive agent, the backtest reviewer and the hypothesis writer.
  • An automation layer drives TradingView for the backtests and the alerts.
  • Every AI action that changes money or promotes a strategy goes through checks written in code.
